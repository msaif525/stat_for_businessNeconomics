Measures of Central Tendency, Variation, and Positional Statistics

1 Demo questions regarding Measures of central tendency and variation

1.1 Practice Problem Statement (Single group’s summary statistics)

Sample Dataset: Monthly Salary (BDT) of 10 Individuals in an area:

15000, 22000, 28000, 32000, 45000, 50000, 60000, 85000, 110000, 250000

Using the sample salary dataset (\(n = 10\)) provided above, solve the following statistical tasks:

Task 1: Measures of Central Tendency

  • Compute the Arithmetic Mean (AM), Geometric Mean (GM), and Harmonic Mean (HM).

  • Compare the three means (AM, GM, and HM) and explain the relationship observed between them (HM≤GM≤AM).

Task 2: Positional Measures & Percentiles

  • Calculate the Median (Q2 ), First Quartile (Q1), and Third Quartile (Q 3).

  • Compute the Interquartile Range (IQR).

  • Calculate the \(80^{th}\) Percentile (\(P_{80}\) ).

  • Determine the threshold values needed to be in:

    • The top 15% of earners (85th percentile, \(P_{85}\)).

  • The bottom 15% of earners (15th percentile, \(P_{15}\)).

Task 3: Measures of Variation

  • Calculate the Sample Standard Deviation.

  • Compute the Coefficient of Variation (CV) to measure relative dispersion in percentage terms.

1.2 Weighted mean

Example 1: Academic Course Grade (GPA Calculation) In a university course, different components of the course carry different weights toward the final grade.

Assessment Component Marks Obtained (\(x_i\)) Weight / Percentage(\(w_i\))
Attendance & Quizzes 85 10% (0.10)
Midterm Exam 72 30% (0.30)
Group Project 90 20% (0.20)
Final Exam 78 40% (0.40)

Calculate the course grade.

Example 2: Average Price of Purchased RiceA family buys different brands/qualities of rice in varying quantities throughout the month. To find the average cost per kilogram, we use the weighted mean (where quantity is the weight \(w_i\) and price per kg is \(x_i\)).

Rice Variety Price per kg (\(x_i\)) in BDT) Quantity Purchased ($w_i$) in kg)
Everyday Miniket 70 20
Premium Nazirshail 90 10
Aromatic Katari 130 5

Calculate the weighted average price per kg of rice purchased.

1.3 Comparative (TWO-group summary statistics comparison)

Example 1 An investment analyst is evaluating six years of historical annual returns (n = 6) for two competing growth funds (Fund X and Fund Y).

Year Fund X Return (%) Fund Y Return (%)
Year 1 +15 +8
Year 2 -5 +2
Year 3 +12 +6
Year 4 -8 -2
Year 5 +18 +10
Year 6 -4 0

a) Compute mean, standard deviation and coefficient of variation of returns fo both funds.

b) Compare coefficient of variation of returns fo both funds. Decide which fund is less risky.

c) If you want to maximize your average return which Fund should you select? Explain.

Example 2: Consistency of Running Times. The following times were recorded by the quarter-mile and mile runners of a university track team (times are in minutes).

Quarter-Mile Times: 0.92, 0.98, 1.04, 0.90, 0.99

Mile Times: 4.52, 4.35, 4.60, 4.70, 4.50

After viewing this sample of running times, one of the coaches commented that the quarter-milers turned in the more consistent times.

i) Use the standard deviation (SD) to compare the variability of type of Timings (Quarter-Mile vs. Mile). Explain which timing is consistent.

ii) Use the coefficient of variation (CV) to compare the variability of type of Timings (Quarter-Mile vs. Mile). Explain which timing is consistent.

iii) In this case which measures of variability (SD vs. CV) is appropriate to compare consistency of timing?

iv) Does the use of the coefficient of variation indicate that the coach’s statement should be qualified?

1.4 Z-scores, Box-plot and outlier detection

1.4.1 Z-score and Chebychev’s inequality

Example-1: Sample Dataset: Monthly Salary (BDT) of 10 Individuals in an area:

15000, 22000, 28000, 32000, 45000, 50000, 60000, 85000, 110000, 250000

  • Compute sample mean and standard deviation of monthly salary.

  • Compute and explain the z-scores of BDT 110000 and BDT 250000.

  • Find the interval which consists at least 80% of salary of the selected individuals.

Example-2: A student scores on a Finance exam where the class mean is 70 and the standard deviation is 8. The same student scores 88 on an Economics exam where the class mean is 80 and the standard deviation is 12.

  • Compute the z-scores for both exam scores.

  • On which exam did the student perform relatively better compared to their peers?

1.4.2 Box-plot and outlier

Sample Dataset: Monthly Salary (BDT) of 10 Individuals in an area:

15000, 22000, 28000, 32000, 45000, 50000, 60000, 85000, 110000, 250000

  1. Compute Q1, Q2, Q3 and IQR.

  2. Report if there is any outlier(s) using \(1.5\times IQR\) rule.

  3. Sketch the box-plot of the salary data along with outlier(s) if any.

  4. Interpret the distribution of salary data from the box-plot.
